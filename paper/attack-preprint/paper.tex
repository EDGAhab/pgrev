\begin{abstract}
Power delivery network (PDN) grid specifications---rail widths, stripe
pitches and offsets, layer connectivity, net phasing---are proprietary
physical-design inputs.  We characterize their identifiability from
layout geometry for regular grid generators, using OpenROAD's
\texttt{pdngen} as the verified vehicle.  A deterministic inference
algorithm recovers every geometry-determining parameter exactly on four
designs across three open PDKs, with wire-for-wire, via-for-via
round-trip regeneration (4/4).  We close the \emph{label} gap: from
de-labeled ODB polygons (per-layer attribution assumed; true imaging
noise remains future work), connectivity tracing plus PDK-anchored
polarity identification recovers the full spec byte-identical to the
labeled pipeline, up to $\mathbb{Z}_2$ VDD/VSS naming symmetry.  A
randomized sweep of 800 configurations reaches 99.5\% dbu-exact
recovery once \texttt{pdngen}'s boundary stripe-dropout rule is modeled
(64 of 66 former failures uniquely resolved; 2 duals flagged
\texttt{AMBIGUOUS}); a proposed (start, offset) duality is rejected
analytically.  Recovery extends to core rings (6/6) and macro grids; an
end-to-end demo replays the recovered spec on a modified floorplan,
regenerating a legal PDN where naive geometry copy shorts.  Defenses:
interleaved decoy grids protect only intent attribution, not geometry;
jitter merely degrades precision; via dropout is falsified.
Stripe-geometry inference transfers to \texttt{addStripe}-style
generators at the parameter-semantics level; via/connect inference does
not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Threat Model}

Hardware reverse engineering (RE) classically proceeds from silicon
upward: delayering, imaging, circuit extraction, and netlist
reconstruction~\cite{torrance2009,quadir2016,fyrbiak2017,botero2021}.  Most RE
research targets logic functionality, while the power delivery network is
treated as background infrastructure.  We show that the PDN deserves
attention in its own right: the \emph{rules} that generate it can be
recovered exactly from the extracted layout database.

\paragraph{Threat model.} The adversary obtains the physical layout
database of a placed-and-routed design (DEF/GDSII/OASIS, e.g.\ from an
untrusted foundry or a leaked tapeout database).  Concretely, our
extractor consumes the post-extraction ODB view: per-shape records carry
the power/ground net identity (from DEF \texttt{SPECIALNETS}
\texttt{USE POWER/GROUND}), the shape kind (\texttt{FOLLOWPIN} vs.\
\texttt{STRIPE}), and via bottom/top layer spans, plus die/core/row
metadata.  No access to the original PDN configuration scripts,
PDK-private design rules beyond the public LEF, or the EDA tool's
internal state is assumed.  The adversary's goal is to recover the PDN
\emph{generation} specification---the parameter bundle sufficient to
reconstruct the grid geometry (whether or not that bundle is accepted
verbatim by the generator's own configuration language)---rather than
merely tracing wires.  We evaluate two input regimes: the labeled
layout-database view above, and de-labeled polygons, for which we add
connectivity tracing and power/ground net identification before
inference runs (\S\ref{sec:unlabeled}).  We state the label situation
plainly: the shape-kind labels in the labeled view are written by the
generator itself, so that view leaks part of the answer; \S\ref{sec:unlabeled}
shows this step is trivially recoverable from geometry (a rule
classifier with 0 misclassifications)---which is exactly what makes the
de-labeled variant meaningful rather than a formality.  What we do
\emph{not} close is the imaging gap: per-layer polygon attribution,
cut-layer grouping, and stack order are assumed known, and delayering
noise (misalignment, missed/false cuts) is unmodeled---true imaged
polygons remain future work.

The spec matters even though the geometry can always be cloned, for two
reasons.  First, \emph{regeneration under modification}: any post-silicon
tampering that must keep the power grid consistent---inserting a hardware
Trojan, re-spinning a block, or porting the layout to a derivative
design---needs the generation rules, not a frozen snapshot of shapes.
The spec is the portable, reusable form of the design IP; the geometry is
one instantiation of it.  Second, and more narrowly, \emph{partial
observation}: an attacker who recovers the rules from a sampled die
region can predict the PDN across the full chip, including areas never
imaged---something static geometry cloning cannot do.  We quantify the
limits of this in \S\ref{sec:crop}: on sky130hd/gcd, a 20\%-area strip
crop or a 10\%-area square crop recovers every parameter exactly and
regenerates the full die, while a thin 10\% strip fails for lack of met4
samples.  Recovery from a region therefore depends on the crop shape and
on each (layer, net) presenting enough period instances; and we stress
these are labeled-database crops---the harder label-free imaged-polygon
setting remains future work.

We instantiate this attack against OpenROAD's \texttt{pdngen}, the
power-grid generator of the open-source OpenROAD
flow~\cite{ajayi2019toward,ajayi2019openroad}.  We chose
\texttt{pdngen} because its source is public, which lets us verify our
forward model line-by-line, and because its rule language
(\texttt{pdngen::specify\_grid}) is representative of industrial PDN
specification styles (rails, stripes, rings, via
connectivity~\cite{kahng2011vlsi}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A verified forward model of \texttt{pdngen} grid generation,
including the stripe-offset reference frame, the boundary
stripe-dropout rule, and the two VSS phasing mechanisms; plus a
deterministic inference algorithm recovering the minimal
geometry-determining grid specification from extracted PG geometry.
\item An empirical identifiability characterization of the
parameter-to-geometry map on the live-parameter quotient space: exact
recovery on 4 designs $\times$ 3 PDKs with round-trip verification,
plus a partial-region study; a randomized sweep of 800 configurations
reaching 99.5\% dbu-exact recovery after dropout modeling, with
explicitly characterized degenerate regimes (phasing coincidence,
single-stripe layers, zero-yield connect pairs, dbu rounding, and two
genuine dropout-induced duals); a proposed (start, offset) duality
rejected analytically.
\item A label-free attack variant: from de-labeled ODB polygons,
connectivity tracing and PDK-anchored net identification recover the
full specification byte-identical to the labeled pipeline, up to a
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ VDD/VSS naming symmetry; plus an end-to-end demo showing
the recovered spec regenerates a legal PDN on a modified floorplan
where naive geometry copy fails.
\item Recovery extended to core rings (6/6 parameters exact) and macro
grids (straps and connect chain exact), each with exact round-trip
regeneration; and a parameter-semantics transfer analysis to Innovus
\texttt{addStripe} (document level---stripe geometry transfers, via/connect
inference does not).
\item An evaluation of anti-RE PDN obfuscations (interleaved decoy grids,
pitch jitter, via dropout) with attacker/defender-side measurements,
including falsified defenses, negative controls, and explicitly
qualitative IR comparisons.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Forward Model of \texttt{pdngen}}

All claims in this section were verified against \texttt{PdnGen.tcl} at
OpenROAD commit \texttt{f12e2f47} (March 2022 build).  We describe the
\texttt{stdcell} grid; macro grids follow the same stripe machinery.

\paragraph{Rails.} One rail is generated per standard-cell row boundary,
with the configured width, spanning the row width.  Net assignment
(VDD/VSS) is determined by row orientation (N rows: VDD on top;
FS rows: flipped---observed on our sky130hd test designs; this follows
the PDK's standard-cell definitions).  Notably, the configuration parameter
\texttt{rails\_start\_with} \emph{appears zero times} in
\texttt{PdnGen.tcl}---it is never read and is therefore a dead
parameter.  Rail \texttt{pitch}/\texttt{offset}, when present, do not
affect rail placement either (a missing pitch defaults to twice the row
height in \texttt{check\_rails}).

\paragraph{Straps.} Let the stdcell area be the union of standard-cell
rows.  \texttt{pdngen} works on an expanded area
(\texttt{stdcell\_plus\_area}): identical in $x$, but extended in $y$ by
half the maximum rail width on each side (the source comment reads
``larger than the stdcell area by half a rail'').  Stripe centers are
computed relative to the reference point
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{ref} = (\mathit{stdcell\_xMin},\;
  \mathit{stdcell\_yMin} - \mathit{max\_rail\_width}/2).
\end{equation}
The base grid (the net named by \texttt{stripes\_start\_with}, POWER
$\rightarrow$ VDD) is placed at $\mathit{ref} + \mathit{offset} + k\cdot
\mathit{pitch}$ for integer $k \geq 0$ while the center is below
$\mathit{area\_max} - \mathit{width}$.  A candidate stripe whose body is
not fully inside the core is \emph{dropped} (leading-stripe dropout):
\texttt{PdnGen.tcl} skips a stripe at center $c$ when
$c - \mathit{width}/2 < \mathit{near} - 1$\,dbu, i.e.\ in practice the
first stripe vanishes when $\mathit{offset} < \mathit{width}/2$
(confirmed by targeted Tcl bisection on both strap layers; an earlier
draft's \texttt{rail\_width}/2 term for met5 was a transcription error
contradicted by the measurements).  The other net's grid is the same
grid shifted by
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{shift} =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathit{pitch}/2, & \text{no \texttt{spacing} given},\\
    \mathit{spacing} + \mathit{width}, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus VDD and VSS each form a complete pitch grid; they are not a single
interleaved grid.  We verified the reference frame numerically, e.g.\
sky130hd/gcd: first VDD met4 stripe center $23690 = 10120 + 13570$ dbu,
and first VDD met5 stripe center $24240 = (10880 - 240) + 13600$ dbu,
where $240$ is half the $480$\,dbu rail width.

\paragraph{Vias and \texttt{connect}.} \texttt{connect} declares logical
layer pairs, e.g.\ \texttt{\{\{met1 met4\} \{met4 met5\}\}}.
\texttt{pdngen} realizes each pair as a via stack spanning all
intermediate layers, placed at same-net wire crossings: a pair may span
several routing layers---asap7's \texttt{\{M1 M5\}} generates a
continuous four-segment stack (M1$\to$M2$\to$M3$\to$M4$\to$M5, 106 vias
per segment).  Whether insertion succeeds is decided not by layer
``adjacency'' but by the geometric overlap of the two endpoint grids at
each crossing: orthogonal (H$\times$V) crossings succeed, while a
parallel narrow rail under a wide stripe, whose overlap cannot cover the
stripe's full width, is skipped entirely with PDN-0042 warnings---which
is why sky130hd's \texttt{\{met1 met5\}} (both horizontal) yields
\emph{zero} vias.  We return to this in Section~\ref{sec:ident}.

\section{Inference Algorithm}

Input: extracted PG geometry (per-shape records: net, layer, shape,
kind $\in$ \{wire, via\}, bounding box; via records carry the bottom/top
layer span), plus die/core/row metadata and the DEF \texttt{SPECIALNETS}
section (to read power/ground net names from \texttt{USE POWER/GROUND}).
The ground-truth configuration is never consulted.  Note this is the
labeled layout-database view described in the threat model---net identity
and shape kinds come from the ODB/DEF metadata, not from unlabeled
polygons.

\paragraph{Rails.} \texttt{FOLLOWPIN} wires are grouped by layer; width is
the modal cross-dimension.  Rail positions are row-determined and need no
further parameters.

\paragraph{Straps.} \texttt{STRIPE} wires are grouped by (net, layer).
Per layer and net, sorted stripe centers are fit by least squares to
$c_i = c_0 + i\cdot p$ (pitch $p$ rounded to the unit grid; residual gate
2\,dbu, which rejects genuinely multimodal inputs loudly rather than
silently accepting them).  The
net with the smaller first center is the base grid, fixing
\texttt{stripes\_start\_with} (POWER or GROUND).  Offset is
$(\text{first base center} - \mathit{ref})$ with $\mathit{ref}$ from
Eq.~(1).  The VSS shift is compared against $\mathit{pitch}/2$: equality
(no \texttt{spacing} emitted) versus $\mathit{spacing} = \mathit{shift} -
\mathit{width}$.

\paragraph{\texttt{connect}.} Via (bottom, top) spans are chained
bottom-to-top through shared layers.  Within each chain, the lowest
\emph{endpoint} rail layer paired with the first strap endpoint above it
yields the rail$\to$strap pair; consecutive strap endpoints within the
chain yield strap$\to$strap pairs.  Intermediate layers of a stack are not
endpoints: in asap7 the chain M1$\to$M2$\to$M3$\to$M4$\to$M5$\to$M6
collapses to \texttt{\{\{M1 M5\} \{M5 M6\}\}}---M2, though a rail layer,
is a middle layer of the M1$\to$M5 stack, so the inferred pair is
M1$\to$M5, matching ground truth.

The output is a minimal grid configuration for \texttt{pdngen}
containing only geometry-determining parameters; dead parameters
(\texttt{halo}, \texttt{rails\_start\_with}, rail pitch/offset, grid name)
are omitted.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We built OpenROAD (\texttt{f12e2f47}, March 2022) and Yosys 0.69
(oss-cad-suite) via conda, with OpenROAD-flow-scripts at commit
\texttt{96eb3de} (April 2022).  Yosys is used only for synthesis; the PDN
geometry under study is independent of the Yosys version.  Four designs were run through floorplan and PDN generation:
\texttt{sky130hd/gcd}, \texttt{nangate45/gcd}, \texttt{sky130hd/aes},
and \texttt{asap7/gcd}.  This ORFS vintage uses the legacy
\texttt{pdngen::specify\_grid} syntax (via \texttt{pdn.cfg} /
\texttt{grid\_strategy} files), which is our inference target.
Geometry was extracted directly from the layout database with the Tcl ODB
API (\texttt{getSWires}/\texttt{getWires} over POWER/GROUND nets),
because this build's \texttt{write\_def} degrades stripe wires to
zero-width points.  None of the four designs contains a core ring or a
macro in its baseline flow; rings and macro grids are evaluated
separately in \S\ref{sec:ringmacro}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All inference and extraction scripts
(\texttt{pg\_infer.py}, the randomized-sweep driver
\texttt{randscan.py}), the ground-truth configurations, and the
\texttt{PdnGen.tcl} source snapshot used for forward-model verification
are public at \url{https://github.com/EDGAhab/pgrev} (MIT license).

\subsection{Variant experiments (sensitivity and dead parameters)}

On sky130hd/gcd we additionally verified: (i) changing met4 pitch
$27.14 \to 30.00$ changes geometry (2,907 $\to$ 2,601 vias) and
re-inference recovers $30.000$ exactly; (ii) flipping
\texttt{stripes\_start\_with} to GROUND swaps the VDD/VSS grids and is
recovered as GROUND; (iii) adding \texttt{rails\_start\_with}=GROUND,
\texttt{halo}=5, and rail pitch/offset leaves geometry bit-identical
(133 wires / 2,907 vias)---direct evidence of dead parameters.

\subsection{Partial-region extrapolation}
\label{sec:crop}

To test the partial-observation motivation, we cropped the
sky130hd/gcd extracted geometry---the labeled ODB view, not imaged
polygons---to three windows and re-ran inference on each crop alone.  A
20\%-width full-height vertical strip ($\sim$20\% die area) recovers every
parameter exactly---met4 1.600/27.140/13.570, met5 1.600/27.200/13.600,
\texttt{connect} and \texttt{stripes\_start\_with} all exact---and
regenerates
the full die: PASS.  A $\sim$10\%-area square in the lower-left corner
(3 stripes per layer per net in-window) likewise recovers everything
exactly: PASS.  But a thin 10\%-width vertical strip fails: met5 is
recovered exactly, yet the window holds only one VDD and zero VSS met4
stripes, so met4 pitch is unidentifiable and full-die regeneration
FAILS---an information deficit, not a numerical error.  Local recovery
therefore depends on crop shape and on each (layer, net) presenting
enough period instances (here, $\geq$2--3 stripes sufficed).  These are
labeled-database crops; the harder label-free imaged-polygon setting is
evaluated next.

\subsection{Label-free attack: from raw polygons to spec}
\label{sec:unlabeled}

The results above assume the labeled ODB view (net identity, shape
kinds, via spans).  We now drop all labels: the input is per-layer
polygons only, simulating a de-labeled layout database.  A six-stage
pipeline restores the labels before the unchanged inference runs:
(1)~connectivity tracing---shapes as nodes, geometric touch/overlap as
edges (same-layer contact, via-to-metal overlap, stacked-via XY
overlap), union-find yielding exactly 2 connected components (1{,}597 /
1{,}443 shapes), each of purity 2/2, in $\sim$2\,s; (2)~PG-net
identification---ranking components by wire count and bbox area (the two
PG nets dominate any signal net; a synthetic test injecting 300 random
signal wires still ranks the two PG nets top-2); (3)~polarity
identification---row orientation plus tap-cell library knowledge
(N rows: VDD rail on top; public PDK information), voting 96/96
rails consistent on sky130hd/gcd and spot-checked on the other three
designs (58/58, 228/228, 106/106 against truth); (4)~rail/stripe
classification---thin horizontal wires on the lowest metal layer are
rails, the rest stripes (0 misclassifications vs.\ truth); (5)~via-span
recovery---cut-layer-to-metal mapping from the process stack plus overlap
checks; (6)~the unmodified \texttt{pg\_infer.py} on the relabeled
geometry.

Every parameter is recovered exactly, including
\texttt{stripes\_start\_with}=POWER; the inferred configuration is
\emph{byte-identical} to the labeled pipeline's output, and the
round-trip regenerates 133/133 wires and 2{,}907/2{,}907 vias.  Two
boundaries.  First, a fundamental one: a global VDD/VSS swap
combined with a \texttt{stripes\_start\_with} flip is geometrically
indistinguishable---a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ \emph{naming} symmetry, broken here
by PDK-anchored rail polarity; an attacker without PDK library knowledge
recovers the spec only up to this $\mathbb{Z}_2$ equivalence class.
Second, the input is a de-labeled ODB, not true imaged polygons:
per-layer polygon attribution, cut-layer grouping, and stack order are
assumed known, and delayering noise (misalignment, missed/false cuts) is
unmodeled---true imaging remains future work.  (The DEF
\texttt{SPECIALNETS} net names used for the final power/ground name
strings are label leakage; geometry recovery does not depend on them.)

\subsection{Core rings and macro grids}
\label{sec:ringmacro}

Core rings and macro grids are the most complex, IP-valuable part of a
PDN spec.  We extend the methodology to both (labeled-database view, as
in \S\S3--5).

\paragraph{Core ring.} On sky130hd/gcd with a chameleon-style
\texttt{core\_ring} truth configuration (met4/met5: width 2.0, spacing
2.0, \texttt{core\_offset} 4.0), ring bars are the unique wire
rectangles whose centers fall outside the core's short-axis range
(stdcell straps extend to the ring's outer edge but stay core-centered).
All six parameters recover exactly (4-side spread 0\,dbu), and the
round-trip regenerates 141/141 wires and 2{,}989/2{,}989 vias.  Inner
ring is VDD, outer VSS (pg\_nets order).  \texttt{pad\_offset} rings were
not covered (no pads in this design).

\paragraph{Macro grid.} On nangate45/gcd with a fakeram45\_32x64 macro
instance, macro straps recover 6/6 exactly (metal5/metal6: width 0.930,
pitch 10.0, offset 2.000 from the macro edge, VSS/VDD order), and the
connect chain \{\texttt{metal4\_PIN\_ver metal5}\}
\{\texttt{metal5 metal6}\} \{\texttt{metal6 metal7}\} is fully recovered
from via evidence (80 pin-to-strap vias identified by their 0.28\,$\mu$m
pin-width via signature).  Round-trip: 84/84 wires, 350/350 vias.
Two boundaries: \texttt{blockages} is only observable up to
superset equivalence (truth \texttt{metal1--4} vs.\ observed
\texttt{metal4}---the round-trip with the reduced set is geometrically
identical, confirming the ambiguity has no geometric consequence), and
pin names plus the \texttt{orient} selector need LEF/DEF, not PDN
geometry.  Rotated macros and the \texttt{\_PIN\_hor} path are untested.

\subsection{End-to-end: the spec as executable intent}
\label{sec:e2e}

\S1 argued the spec's value over frozen geometry is \emph{regeneration
under modification}; we now demonstrate it end-to-end.  Starting from
the \emph{unlabeled} pipeline's recovered configuration for the sky130hd
gcd design---\texttt{pdn\_inferred.cfg}, holding rails met1 w0.480;
straps met4 w1.600, p27.140, o13.570 and met5 w1.600, p27.200, o13.600;
connect pairs met1--met4 and met4--met5; \texttt{stripes\_start\_with}
POWER---we modified the floorplan by inserting a $50\times50\,\mu$m
fake macro (\texttt{FAKE\_MACRO\_0}) at the core center and replayed
\texttt{pdngen} with the recovered stdcell spec reused verbatim, plus
only the macro-grid declaration a designer would standardly write for
the new floorplan (\texttt{orient}, \texttt{blockages} "met4 met5",
halo)---which carries no stripe geometry.  The run completes with 0
errors and no PDN-0042 or PDN-0079 warnings, producing a legal,
self-avoiding PDN: 141 wires / 2{,}785 vias; 8 stripes intersecting the
macro are each cut into two segments whose 16 ends stop exactly at the
halo boundary; all 163 surviving met4$\times$met5 crossings carry their
via4 stack (0 missing); 0 wires and 0 vias enter the macro rectangle.
The control---naively copying the original 3{,}040 polygons onto the new
floorplan (die and core unchanged, the most favorable case for
copying)---leaves 8 stripes shorting straight through the macro and 122
via rectangles landing inside it (46 distinct stack positions, 8 of them
dead met4--met5 stacks).  No rigid transform can legalize all 3{,}040
shapes at once, and hand-repairing cut stripes and dead vias does not
scale with design size; parametric regeneration is the only systematic
route.  The thesis in one line: \emph{the spec is executable intent;
the geometry is a one-time instance.}  Boundaries: this is replay
inside the same generator, not cross-tool migration; the macro-grid
declaration is designer-standard rather than inferred (the original
design has no macro, so no macro-rule information exists in its
geometry); and met1 rails still pass under the macro, since
\texttt{blockages} covers stripe layers only, consistent with the
platform's macro-grid semantics.

\section{Identifiability Analysis}
\label{sec:ident}

We began this study expecting the parameter$\to$geometry mapping to be
significantly non-injective, hoping to characterize large equivalence
classes.  The experiments say otherwise.  Formally, let $G$ be the map
from \texttt{pdngen} configurations to placed grid geometry, and let
$\sim$ identify configurations that differ only in dead parameters:
\texttt{rails\_start\_with} (dead by exhaustive source
inspection---a full-text search of \texttt{PdnGen.tcl} at
\texttt{f12e2f47}, archived in our public repo, finds zero occurrences;
configuration keys are accessed only as literals and \texttt{dict keys}
enumerates layer names, excluding dynamic-key indirection; the parameter
no longer exists in the current C++ \texttt{src/pdn/}), \texttt{halo}
(macro-only), rail \texttt{pitch}/\texttt{offset} (dead by code
inspection and case verification: re-adding them leaves geometry
bit-identical), and the arbitrary grid name.
\textbf{On the quotient space of live parameters, $G$ is empirically
identifiable}---every live parameter was recovered exactly on all tested
configurations---\textbf{except in the following degenerate regimes},
which we state explicitly as the non-trivial equivalence cases observed:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Phasing coincidence.} When an explicit \texttt{spacing} $S$
and width $W$ satisfy $S + W = \mathit{pitch}/2$, the resulting VSS phase
coincides with the no-\texttt{spacing} default, so the explicit-spacing
and no-spacing configurations are geometrically identical.
\item \emph{Single-stripe layers.} A layer carrying exactly one stripe
leaves \texttt{pitch} unidentifiable: any pitch above a geometry-implied
lower bound reproduces the same shapes.
\item \emph{Zero-yield connect pairs.} A \texttt{connect} pair that
produces no vias is geometrically equivalent to omitting that pair
entirely.  The mechanism is geometric overlap, not layer adjacency
(\S2): the spanning pair \texttt{\{\{met1 met5\}\}} yields \emph{zero}
vias because the parallel narrow met1 rail cannot cover the wide met5
stripe---\texttt{pdngen} skips every insertion (PDN-0042)---while the
split pair \texttt{\{\{met1 met4\} \{met4 met5\}\}} yields the full
2,907-via stack, so spanning-vs-split is identifiable (connect-pair
granularity is recoverable), but spanning-vs-omitted is a genuine
equivalence.  Note this zero-yield behavior was observed on the 2022
legacy Tcl implementation; the current C++ version turns it into a hard
error (PDN-0179, see \S\ref{sec:deflim}).
\item \emph{Micron-to-dbu rounding.} Stripe geometry is computed in
database units, so distinct $\mu$m values that round to the same dbu are
indistinguishable.  Recovery is therefore exact only down to the layout
database's unit grid, and parameter values must be quoted with that
quantization in mind.
\item \emph{Dropout-induced naming duality.} When the leading stripe is
dropped (\S2), which net anchors the base grid can become
unidentifiable: two hypotheses---(POWER base, $k{=}1$ dropped) vs.\
(GROUND base, $k{=}1$ dropped)---generate dbu-identical geometry.  Two
such cases were observed in the sweep and are flagged
\texttt{AMBIGUOUS} by the inference (\S\ref{sec:sweep}).
\end{enumerate}

Outside these regimes, every geometry-determining parameter was recovered
exactly from the extracted layout database on every tested
configuration.  Macro-grid parameters, pin names, and blockages are
unrecoverable only when absent from the design (no macros, hence no
macro-grid geometry to observe).  We deliberately weaken ``injective''
to \emph{empirically identifiable}: the joint transforms we did not test
could in principle add equivalence cases, and we state the evidence
scale explicitly below.

\paragraph{Randomized sweep: 800 configurations.}
\label{sec:sweep}
To move beyond four hand-picked designs, we swept random
\texttt{pdngen} configurations on sky130hd/gcd
(width/pitch/offset/spacing/\texttt{stripes\_start\_with} sampled over
legal ranges; real \texttt{pdngen} runs).  The first pass recovered
732/800 = 91.5\% dbu-exact; all 66 inference failures traced to one
cause---\texttt{pdngen} drops any stripe not fully inside the core
(\S2), flipping the lowest visible stripe's net and tripping the
cross-layer \texttt{starts\_with} consistency assertion.  Rather than
excusing this as a modeling gap, we closed it: the inference now runs a
dropout-aware correction loop---naive least-squares fit, then forward
validation with a dbu-exact forward model \emph{including} the dropout
rule, then hypothesis search over (base net, number $k$ of dropped
leading stripes), keeping every globally cross-layer-consistent
hypothesis and flagging \texttt{AMBIGUOUS} when more than one survives.
Re-running the full $N=800$ sweep: inference succeeds on
\textbf{800/800}, geometric round-trip passes on \textbf{800/800}, and
\textbf{796/800 = 99.5\%} recover every parameter exactly, with
\textbf{0 failures}.  Of the 66 former failures, 64 resolve to a
\emph{unique} globally consistent hypothesis---which must then be the
truth (the sound forward model guarantees the truth is among the
enumerated hypotheses, so uniqueness implies identity; each is confirmed
by bit-exact round-trip).  We state the verification honestly: the
original sweep's truth records were lost with ephemeral scratch
storage, so these 64 rely on the uniqueness argument plus round-trip
confirmation rather than item-by-item truth comparison.  The remaining 2
(\texttt{ws2\_0082}, \texttt{ws2\_0507}) are genuine geometric duals---two
globally consistent hypotheses generating dbu-identical geometry---and
are flagged \texttt{AMBIGUOUS}; two further equivalences carried over
from the pre-fix scan bring the total to 4/800.  Width and pitch recover
with zero error on all 800.  One methodological lesson: the
default-vs-explicit \texttt{spacing} decision must use \emph{exact}
integer comparison ($\mathit{shift} == \mathit{pitch}//2$); a tolerance
misclassified a 1\,dbu deviation and shifted a whole net---1\,dbu is not
noise on the parameter side.  (The dropout threshold is
$\mathit{offset} < \mathit{width}/2$ on both strap layers, confirmed by
targeted Tcl bisection; an independent pre-fix scan with $N=630$ gave
consistent 92.2\%.)

\paragraph{The $(\neg\mathit{start},\,o+\mathit{shift})$ duality does not
exist (analytic).} A natural equivalence hypothesis---(POWER, offset
$o$) vs.\ (GROUND, offset $o+\mathit{shift}$)---fails by construction of
the loop bound: (POWER, $o$) always places the boundary stripe at
$\mathit{ref}+o$ for $o \geq 0$, while the shifted dual necessarily loses
it (typical difference: 2 wires $+$ 169 vias).  For $o \geq 0$ the
proposed equivalence class cannot exist, so \texttt{starts\_with} and
offset are jointly identifiable.  We confirmed the direction empirically
by generating the literal dual of 553 exactly recovered configurations
(0 identical), but the argument itself is analytic, not statistical.

\paragraph{Equivalence-class catalog.} The sweep confirms four
non-trivial classes: \textbf{P} (explicit \texttt{spacing} with
$S+W=\mathit{pitch}/2$ vs.\ omitting it---20/20 round-trip PASS);
\textbf{S} ($\mu$m values rounding to the same dbu collide---30/30
$\pm$0.35\,dbu variants geometrically identical); the
\textbf{single-stripe} boundary (pitch genuinely unidentifiable with one
stripe per net); and the \textbf{dropout-induced naming duality} (2
cases, flagged \texttt{AMBIGUOUS}).  A hypothesized offset-translation
class $(sw,o)$ vs.\ $(sw,o+p)$ is falsified (finite core loses exactly
one boundary stripe).

\section{Defenses: Anti-RE PDN Obfuscation}
\label{sec:defense}

The attack succeeds because rule-generated PDNs are perfectly regular:
uniform pitches, deterministic VSS phasing, and complete via stacks at
every crossing make the forward model invertible.  A natural counter is
\emph{anti-RE PDN obfuscation}: grids engineered so that the inference
assumptions fail, while the PDN remains functional.  Hardware obfuscation
has a rich literature at the logic level (camouflaging, logic
locking~\cite{shamsi2019,rajendran2012}), in split
manufacturing~\cite{perez2020,imeson2013}, and in design-for-trust
generally~\cite{rajendran2014}; physical-design
watermarking~\cite{kahng2001} and power-signature
watermarking~\cite{ziener2008} hide information in layout and power
networks respectively, and layout-level anti-RE
techniques~\cite{chen2015} are the closest defense-side analog to our
decoy grids.  On the attack side, our work is most directly preceded by
ReGDS~\cite{rajarathnam2020}, which recovers gate-level netlists from
GDSII at 100\%---we invert the same ``layout as evidence'' threat model
one level up, recovering generator \emph{parameters} rather than
netlists---and the power grid's security relevance is independently
established by power-grid-aware side-channel
analysis~\cite{wang2013} and PDN-impedance tamper
sensing~\cite{mosavirik2023}.  A systematic search found no peer-reviewed
prior work on obfuscation of the on-chip PDN structure itself.
(Search scope: IEEE Xplore and ACM Digital Library, 2000--2026,
keywords ``PDN/power grid obfuscation,'' ``power grid anti-reverse
engineering,'' ``PDN camouflaging''; hardware-security venues
HOST, CHES, DAC, ICCAD, DATE, TCAD, TVLSI; we claim absence from this
retrieval, not a proof of non-existence.)
(Prior metal-mesh work targets invasive probing and tamper
evidence---conductive shield layers that detect decapsulation---a
different threat model from rule extraction, which we do not count as
PDN-grid obfuscation prior art.)  On the synthesis side, the EDA
literature addresses the \emph{forward} problem---sizing and optimizing
power grids under IR-drop and electromigration constraints
(textbook treatment in~\cite{kahng2011vlsi} and the DAC/TCAD
PDN-synthesis literature); we found no work inverting a production
generator's rule parameters from placed layout geometry, which is the
inverse problem studied here.  On the inference side, prior RE work
exploits layout regularity mostly to recover \emph{instances}, not
generator parameters: Degate recognizes standard cells by approximate
template matching against a library of known layout patterns, and
ML-based gate recognition classifies imaged structures---both stop at
identifying what is on the silicon, without asking which \emph{rules}
produced it.  Our work inverts one level further: from placed geometry
back to the generator's rule parameters.  On the industrial side,
parametric PDN templates are standard practice, which is why the
transfer question matters: Synopsys ICC2's
\texttt{create\_pg\_mesh\_pattern} (per-layer width/spacing/pitch/offset/trim)
and Cadence Innovus's \texttt{addStripe} power-planning flow both express
the grid as a reusable parameter template---precisely the class of object
our geometry$\to$bundle inference consumes (\S\ref{sec:deflim}).

\paragraph{Defense goal and methodology.} The defender's goal is to make
the grid \emph{specification}---not the geometry, which an attacker can
always clone---unrecoverable or ambiguous, while keeping the PDN
functional: fully connected, DRC-clean, and with no significant IR-drop
degradation.  Naming: we label experiments E1--E3 and the underlying
defense mechanisms D1--D4 (design-doc numbering)---D1: decoys without
vias or net attachment, D2: pitch jitter, D3: interleaved decoy grids,
D4: via dropout---so E1 evaluates D3, E2 evaluates D2, and E3 evaluates
D4.  We denote the concrete interleaved-decoy instance evaluated here
(36 extra via'd stripes at $+P/4$) E1a.  We evaluate candidate obfuscations on sky130hd/gcd against
two attacker models: the naive deterministic inference of
Section~3 (zero modifications), and adaptive attackers that know a
defense may be present.  Defender-side checks are: (i) connectivity of
the wire/via graph per net (a single component, no dangling vias); (ii)
LEF minimum spacing; (iii) a lumped resistive mesh (LEF
\texttt{RPERSQ}, uniform 1\,A rail-current injection, ideal met5 feed,
5\,$\Omega$ nominal via resistance) used strictly as a
\emph{comparative} IR metric, never as signoff.  Experiments manipulate
the extracted geometry directly (the complete input of
\texttt{pg\_infer.py}), which is equivalent to ODB-level edits for every
attacker-side measurement; the unmodeled gap is that signal-wire DRC around
decoys is not modeled, though decoys sit in the $+P/4$ gaps
($5.19\,\mu\mathrm{m}$) far from any DRC risk region, with LEF spacing
rules verified.

\subsection{E1: Interleaved decoy grids (effective)}

\paragraph{Setup.} On sky130hd/gcd (met4 pitch $27140$\,dbu, met5 pitch
$27200$\,dbu), every true stripe is duplicated at $+P/4$ with the same
width, the same net, and a full copy of its via stack (met1--met4 stack
plus the met4--met5 crossing vias), faithfully simulating a second
\texttt{pdngen} pass interleaved with the first; decoy$\times$decoy
same-net met4--met5 vias are added as well.  Stripes obeying
\texttt{pdngen}'s loop bound yield 36 extra decoy stripes (18 per layer):
wires $133 \to 169$, vias $2{,}907 \to 5{,}994$.

\paragraph{Attacker side.} The naive inference fails loudly: with the
least-squares fit of \S3, met4 VDD reports pitch $13570.0$\,dbu with a
maximum residual of $3571.1$\,dbu, far above the 2\,dbu gate
(\texttt{AssertionError}; no parameters emitted).  The exact-recovery
attack of Sections~3--5 is defeated outright---for the naive attacker.

\paragraph{Adaptive attacker: multi-sequence inference.} An attacker who
knows decoys may be present upgrades the pitch fit to $k$ arithmetic
sequences per (layer, net): $P$ from the modal adjacent difference,
phases from circular clustering of $(c \bmod P)$, each cluster validated
as an arithmetic progression.  With $k=2$ this attacker recovers
\emph{all 73 stripe centers exactly}---met4 $P = 27.140\,\mu\mathrm{m}$
with phases at truth and truth$+P/4$ (19/19 VDD, 18/18 VSS centers), met5
$P = 27.200\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ likewise (18/18 per net).  The prediction is
confirmed: both attack goals of Section~1 (extrapolation, regeneration
under modification) need only this parameter bundle, not knowledge of
which sequence is original.  D3 therefore does \emph{not} prevent
geometry cloning.\footnote{Strictly, this parameter bundle is not a
single legacy-\texttt{pdngen} configuration: feeding two
\texttt{specify\_grid stdcell} blocks silently ignores the second (one
\texttt{PDN-0013} in the log; first-writer-wins), and running
\texttt{pdngen} twice rips up the first grid.  E1a's interleaved geometry
was constructed geometrically, which is all the attacker needs---the
bundle regenerates shapes directly.}

\paragraph{What D3 does protect: intent attribution.} Even with the full
two-sequence bundle recovered, the attacker cannot determine which
sequence is the designer's original grid.  Enumerating stripe subsets on
met4 VSS (9 true + 9 decoy stripes) yields two \emph{exactly equivalent}
9-stripe pitch grids---the true grid and the decoy grid.  On VDD (10 true
+ 9 decoy), three 9-stripe candidates exist (two true 9-subsequences plus
the decoy grid); the full 10-stripe grid is unique, but identifying it
requires the prior knowledge that the original had 10 stripes, which the
attacker does not have.  A defender could in principle invert the
majority (more decoy than true stripes) so that ``largest AP is the
original'' heuristics fail---we did not implement or test this; it
remains an untested hypothesis.  This is \textbf{genuine
intent ambiguity}: two stripe subsets both form valid
\texttt{pdngen}-style pitch grids, and the attacker cannot determine
which was the designer's original.  (This is a different object from the
configuration-level equivalence classes hunted in
Section~\ref{sec:ident}---there, two generator configurations mapping to
the same geometry; here, two subsets of one defended geometry each
admitting a valid generator configuration.)  In sum: D3
blinds the naive attacker and reduces the adaptive attacker from exact
\emph{attribution} to ambiguity; it does not impede geometry recovery at
all.  We state the value of this narrowly: intent attribution matters
only if the attacker's objective requires the designer's \emph{original}
intent rather than any functionally equivalent grid (e.g.\ tampering
that must stay consistent with the defender's signoff baseline); for the
two primary attack goals of Section~1---extrapolation and regeneration
under modification---it offers nothing.

\paragraph{Defender side.} VDD and VSS each remain a single connected
component with zero dangling vias.  Minimum stripe spacing is
$5.185\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ on met4 ($\geq 0.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ LEF) and
$5.2\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ on met5 ($\geq 1.6\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ LEF).  The mesh
reports worst-case drop $66.90 \to 25.98$\,mV/A and mean $38.89 \to
11.63$\,mV/A---but this $-61.2\%$ is \emph{fully explained by doubled
metal}, not by obfuscation.  Against an equal-metal unobfuscated baseline
(met4/met5 pitch halved to $P/2$, no decoys; 169 wires, 5,994 vias,
stripe length $18{,}918.4\,\mu\mathrm{m}$---identical to E1a):

\subsection{E2: Pitch jitter (degrades precision, does not blind)}

\paragraph{Setup.} Each met4 stripe and its full via stack are translated
by $\delta \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(-j, +j)$ for $j \in \{0, 2\%, 5\%, 10\%,
15\%\}$ of pitch, three seeds each (15 attempts).  Stripes violating LEF
spacing or via landing (vias must stay within the rail/met5
perpendicular overlap extent) are resampled---2 runs at $j=15\%$---so
the 13 final runs are all physically legal.

\paragraph{Attacker side.} The naive inference breaks at $j \geq 2\%$
(its $\pm 2$\,dbu arithmetic-sequence assertion is far exceeded).  But a
$\sim$30-line robust least-squares pitch fit---the obvious attacker
upgrade---still recovers pitch with mean relative error $0\%$,
$0.14\%$, $0.42\%$, $0.40\%$, $0.50\%$ (max $0\%$, $0.21\%$, $0.76\%$,
$0.97\%$, $1.19\%$) at $j = 0\%, 2\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%$ ($j=0\%$ is the
unmodified pipeline self-check), and offset within
$2.97\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ at $15\%$.  Jitter's ceiling is therefore
``degrades precision,'' not ``blinds'': it converts exact recovery into
approximate recovery, which may suffice for a foundry cloning the grid
but not for hiding the design intent.

\paragraph{Defender side.} All 13 runs keep a single connected component
per net, zero dangling vias, minimum spacing $\geq 5.68\,\mu\mathrm{m}$,
and mesh worst-case drop essentially unchanged from baseline (within the
coarse model's noise; we claim only ``electrically free'' qualitatively,
not a precise percentage)---jitter is electrically free, but buys little security against an adaptive attacker.

\subsection{E3: Via dropout (falsified as a defense)}

Deleting $p \in \{10\%, 30\%, 50\%\}$ of the 912 rail$\to$strap via
stacks (2,736 vias) leaves \texttt{connect} inference \emph{unchanged}
(\texttt{\{\{met1 met4\} \{met4 met5\}\}}): recovery operates on via
\emph{span types}, not instances, so instance deletion cannot move the
attacker's output---while mesh worst-case drop degrades by up to
$+426\%$.  Deleting the entire met2--met3 span does corrupt
\texttt{connect} (mis-inferred as \texttt{\{\{met4 met5\}\}}), but it
fragments the PDN into 49 components per net and disconnects all 192
rail injection nodes; an analytic projection shows the mis-inferred
configuration would regenerate with zero rail$\to$strap stacks.  Via
dropout is self-defeating: a falsified defense, kept only because it
quantifies that connect identifiability depends on via-chain integrity
(Section~\ref{sec:ident}).

\subsection{Negative controls}
\label{sec:neg}

We report three weak mechanisms rather than hiding them.
\textbf{Floating decoys} (stripes with no net) never enter inference at
all: our extractor iterates only POWER/GROUND nets, so they are filtered
before inference begins.  \textbf{Via-less decoys} (same net, no vias)
crash the naive inference, but a one-line adaptive filter---dropping
stripes with no vias---restores \emph{exact} recovery of every
parameter, so this defense is falsified.  \textbf{Half-pitch ($+P/2$)
decoys} are physically unrealizable under the no-spacing phasing used
here: the VSS grid sits exactly at
$+P/2$ from VDD, so such decoys coincide with the opposite net's stripe
centers---a hard short---and were never run.

Limitations must be stated plainly.  The resistive mesh is a comparative
first-order model (uniform current, ideal met5 feed, nominal
$5\,\Omega$ vias, no packaging or EM analysis)---it cannot be presented
as signoff, and no commercial IR-drop/EM signoff is available in this
environment.  The routability cost of D3 is now quantified against an equal-metal
baseline: $2\times$ routing blockage on met4/met5 and $2.06\times$ via
count, with no IR benefit over the unobfuscated $P/2$ grid in our coarse
model; a full-flow routing congestion/timing measurement remains future work.  The
main attack experiments (\S3--\S5) use a toy design (gcd) and OpenROAD
\texttt{f12e2f47} (2022) with the legacy Tcl \texttt{pdngen}
(\texttt{pdngen::specify\_grid}).  Only three distinct hand-picked grid
configurations are exercised (sky130hd/gcd and aes share one), but the
randomized sweep (\S\ref{sec:sweep}) covers 800 configurations, and core
rings and macro grids are covered in \S\ref{sec:ringmacro};
\texttt{pad\_offset} rings and rotated macros remain future work.
Commercial generators: we have no license for tool measurement, so we
analyzed transferability at the parameter-semantics level instead.
Innovus \texttt{addStripe} (documented in public tutorials and
community sources) generates the same geometric object class our
inference consumes---a periodic set of equal-width parallel stripes over
a rectangular area, one stripe per listed net per set---and its options
map onto our geometry-sufficient bundle almost 1:1:
\texttt{-width}, \texttt{-set\_to\_set\_distance},
\texttt{-start\_offset}, \texttt{-spacing}, \texttt{-nets},
\texttt{-direction}, \texttt{-layer} and \texttt{-area} play the roles
of width, pitch, offset, spacing, net polarity, direction, layer and
generation domain respectively.  The
geometry$\to$bundle stage of the attack is therefore tool-agnostic in
principle; the bundle$\to$config stage is a table lookup.  The hard
boundary is via/connect inference, which does \emph{not} transfer:
\texttt{addStripe} draws stripes only, while vias are generated by a
separate \texttt{sroute} step with its own parameters, so our via-stack
and \texttt{connect} recovery stays \texttt{pdngen}-specific.  Datum
conventions (edge vs.\ centerline), grid snapping, and boundary
truncation are unmeasured and not claimed.  Verdict: transferability
analyzed at parameter-semantics level; tool measurement is future work.
The defenses D2/D3 were evaluated as geometry post-processing steps, not
through a modified PDN flow---deploying them in practice requires flow
integration (decoy insertion inside \texttt{pdngen} or as a post-PDN ECO
step), which we did not implement.  To check that the exact-recovery
mechanism is not a legacy-only artifact, we rebuilt current OpenROAD from
source (HEAD \texttt{80c6be9}, September 2026) and re-ran the full
round-trip on \texttt{sky130hd/gcd} under the new C++ API
(\texttt{define\_pdn\_grid} / \texttt{add\_pdn\_stripe} /
\texttt{add\_pdn\_connect}), using the 2022 ORFS sky130hd LEF as input
data.  The inferred configuration regenerates the truth geometry
\emph{exactly}: 3,051/3,051 shapes (134 wires $+$ 2,917 vias), zero
symmetric difference, with met4/met5 widths, pitches, and offsets
recovered to the unit grid (met4: 1.600/27.140/13.570; met5:
1.600/27.200/13.600).  To preempt confusion: both versions' ground-truth
configurations use the \emph{same} offset parameter values (met5 offset
13.6), and the recovered 13.600 in each case is relative to that
version's reference frame---legacy $(10120, 10640)$\,dbu from Eq.~(1)
vs.\ the current version's core-area corner $(10120, 10880)$\,dbu, a
240\,dbu shift in $y$.  The inference forward model is adapted per
version, so ``exact recovery'' means exact recovery of the parameter
\emph{values}; the 133$\to$134 wire difference is the geometric
consequence of the same values under the shifted datum (one stripe moves
in or out of the loop bound).  The 3{,}051/3{,}051 figure is a
within-new-version round-trip: regeneration with the inferred
configuration compared against the new version's own extracted geometry.  A successful \texttt{\{met1 met4\}} connect now
yields a \emph{complete} intermediate via stack (912 vias per
met1--met2, met2--met3, and met3--met4 step, 181 for met4--met5).
Generator quirks differ from the 2022 version in detail: the
non-adjacent \texttt{\{met1 met5\}} connect is now a hard error
([ERROR PDN-0179] ``unable to repair all channels'') rather than a
silent zero-via skip; \texttt{-verbose} and \texttt{-pin\_direction}
are explicitly marked deprecated (parsed but with no geometric effect);
\texttt{add\_pdn\_stripe -width 0} is still rejected (PDN-0106, minimum
width $0.3000\,\mu\mathrm{m}$).  The current-version re-run therefore
confirms the forward model and the exact-recovery mechanism, while the
defense experiments (E1--E3) remain validated on the 2022 version only.
Finally, these defenses protect the grid \emph{specification} (design IP)
against rule extraction; they do not stop an attacker who simply clones
the geometry---a different threat model.  We also note the attack surface
widens on commercial generators, which our test set does not cover by
measurement.

\section{Conclusion}

We characterized the identifiability of a regular PDN generator's rule
parameters from layout geometry, using OpenROAD's \texttt{pdngen} as the
verified vehicle.  The parameter-to-geometry map proved empirically
identifiable on the live-parameter quotient space: exact recovery on
four designs across three PDKs with wire-for-wire, via-for-via
round-trip regeneration (4/4), confirmed not to be a legacy-only artifact
by an exact round-trip on current OpenROAD (HEAD \texttt{80c6be9}); a
randomized sweep of 800 configurations reaches 99.5\% dbu-exact recovery
once the boundary stripe-dropout rule is modeled, with the remaining
cases falling into explicitly characterized degenerate regimes---a
somewhat surprising negative result for the equivalence-class hunt we
began with.  We closed the label gap (de-labeled ODB polygons suffice,
up to a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ naming symmetry; true imaging noise remains future
work), extended recovery to core rings and macro grids, and demonstrated
end-to-end that the recovered spec regenerates a legal PDN on a modified
floorplan where naive geometry copy fails---the spec is executable
intent, the geometry a one-time instance.  For defenders, the takeaway
is negative: regular PDN grids leak their complete construction rules,
and this leakage can be engineered into \emph{intent} ambiguity at
best---interleaved decoy grids leave an adaptive attacker unable to tell
which of two equivalent grids is original, though this matters only when
the attacker needs the designer's original intent---but not into geometry
unclonability, and not for free (no IR benefit over an equal-metal
unobfuscated grid); cheap fixes (jitter, via dropout) do not suffice.
For the open-source EDA community, the verified forward model (now
including the dropout rule) and the dead-parameter findings (notably
\texttt{rails\_start\_with}, dead by exhaustive source inspection) are
directly actionable.
