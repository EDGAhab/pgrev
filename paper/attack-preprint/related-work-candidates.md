# Related-Work Candidates for "Exact Recovery of PDN Grid Specifications from Layout Geometry"

Literature search completed 2026-09-27. All entries are real publications verified against publisher pages, DOI records, IACR cryptodb, arXiv, or reputable indexes. Items that could not be fully verified are marked "待核实".

---

## 1. Layout-level / chip-level reverse engineering (delayering, imaging, netlist extraction)

- **The State-of-the-Art in IC Reverse Engineering** — Randy Torrance, Dick James. *CHES 2009 (Invited paper)*, 2009, pp. 363–381. Practitioner's overview of destructive RE pipeline (depackaging, delayering, SEM imaging, circuit extraction) from industry reverse engineers; the canonical citation for the delayering+imaging workflow. [DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-04138-9_26](https://www.iacr.org/cryptodb/data/paper.php?pubkey=18605)
- **A Survey on Chip to System Reverse Engineering** — Shahed E. Quadir, Junlin Chen, Domenic Forte, Navid Asadizanjani, Sina Shahbazmohamadi, Lei Wang, John Chandy, Mark Tehranipoor. *ACM JETC* 13(1):6, 2016. Survey of chip- and board-level RE techniques plus a section on anti-RE (camouflaging, dummy contacts, transformable interconnects). [listing](https://dforte.ece.ufl.edu/publications/)
- **Hardware Trust and Assurance through Reverse Engineering: A Tutorial and Outlook from Image Analysis and Machine Learning Perspectives** — Ulbert J. Botero, Ronald Wilson, Hangwei Lu, Mir T. Rahman, Mukhil A. Mallaiyan, Fatemeh Ganji, Navid Asadizanjani, Mark M. Tehranipoor, Damon L. Woodard, Domenic Forte. *ACM JETC* 17(4):1–53, 2021. Image-analysis + ML survey of automated RE; the closest prior framing of "automated extraction of design intent from layout data," which our geometry-to-spec inversion extends to parametric PDN specifications. [DOI 10.1145/3464959](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3464959)
- **Hardware Reverse Engineering: Overview and Open Challenges** — Marc Fyrbiak, Sebastian Strauß, Christian Kison, Sebastian Wallat, Malte Elson, Nikol Rummel, Christof Paar. *IEEE IVSW* 2017, pp. 88–94. Documents the foundry-vs-end-user threat models and open quantification problems in RE; useful for positioning our exact-recovery attack model against the broader RE threat landscape. [DOI 10.1109/IVSW.2017.8031550](https://doi.org/10.1109/IVSW.2017.8031550)
- **Degate (reverse engineering integrated circuits with Degate)** — Martin Schobert et al. Open-source tool, not a peer-reviewed paper. Automated IC RE tool from optical/SEM imagery; cited here as the representative open-source baseline of the tooling our attack replaces/supersedes for the PDN sub-problem.

## 2. GDSII-to-netlist recovery

- **ReGDS: A Reverse Engineering Framework from GDSII to Gate-level Netlist** — Rachel Selina Rajarathnam, Yibo Lin, Yier Jin, David Z. Pan. *IEEE HOST* 2020, pp. 154–163. The direct ancestor of our attack: uses GDSII layout + technology library to extract transistor-level connectivity and recover the gate-level netlist at 100%, even on XOR/MUX-locked circuits. Our work applies the same "inside-foundry from layout" threat model but inverts grid-generation *parameters* rather than extracting logic. [NSF PAR 10237619](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10237619-regds-reverse-engineering-framework-from-gdsii-gate-level-netlist)
- **Large-Area Automated Layout Extraction Methodology for Full-IC Reverse Engineering** — Raul Quijada, Roger Dura, Jofre Pallares, Xavier Formatje, Salvador Hidalgo, Francisco Serra-Graells. *Journal of Hardware and Systems Security* 2(4):322–332, 2018. Full-chip-scale layout-to-circuit extraction methodology; complements ReGDS with a scaled industrial workflow.
- **Netlist Reverse Engineering for High-Level Functionality Reconstruction** — Travis Meade, Shaojie Zhang, Yier Jin. *ASP-DAC* 2016, pp. 655–660. Netlist-level RE for high-level function reconstruction; demonstrates that extracted connectivity can be lifted to design intent, supporting the claim that layout parameters are equally extractable design intent.
- **Chip-Level Anti-Reverse Engineering Using Transformable Interconnects** — Shuren Chen, Junlin Chen, Domenic Forte, Jia Di, Mark Tehranipoor, Lei Wang. *IEEE DFTS* 2015, pp. 109–114. Defense-side paper where the interconnect/anti-RE approach is a layout-level analog to our decoy-grid defense; verified via Forte's UFL publication list and UConn CHEST research page.

## 3. PDN / power-grid watermarking and fingerprinting

- **Power Signature Watermarking of IP Cores for FPGAs** — Daniel Ziener, Jürgen Teich. *Journal of Signal Processing Systems* 51(1):123–136, 2008. Embeds an IP watermark that is decoded from a power-supply voltage trace; the classic "information encoded in/on the power network" precedent. [DOI 10.1007/s11265-007-0136-8](https://cris.fau.de/publications/111926804)
- **Multiplexing Methods for Power Watermarking** — Daniel Ziener, Florian Baueregger, Jürgen Teich. *IEEE HOST* 2010. Robustness techniques (code-division multiplexing, optical orthogonal codes) for power watermarks under noise; relevant to distinguishing decoy vs. true grids in power-domain analysis.
- **Constraint-Based Watermarking Techniques for Design IP Protection** — Andrew B. Kahng, John Lach, William H. Mangione-Smith, Stefanus Mantik, Igor L. Markov, Miodrag Potkonjak, Paul Tucker, Huijuan Wang, Gregory Wolfe. *IEEE TCAD* 20(10):1236–1252, 2001. Foundational physical-design watermarking: embeds watermarks as constraints in placement/routing solutions, with proof-of-authorship metrics; closest IP-protection analog to hiding/decoying layout structure. [DOI 10.1109/43.952740](https://oa.mg/author/andrew-b-kahng-A5073558386)
- **Signature Hiding Techniques for FPGA Intellectual Property Protection** — John Lach, William H. Mangione-Smith, Miodrag Potkonjak. *ICCAD* 1998, pp. 186–189. Early FPGA watermarking work; establishes the concealment-vs-detection tradeoff framing reused in our decoy-grid discussion.
- **FPGA Core Watermarking Based on Power Signature Analysis** — Daniel Ziener, Jürgen Teich. *IEEE FPT* 2006, pp. 205–212. Conference predecessor of the 2008 journal power-watermarking paper.

## 4. Hardware Trojan detection via PDN / power side channels

- **A Survey of Hardware Trojan Taxonomy and Detection** — Mohammad Tehranipoor, Farinaz Koushanfar. *IEEE Design & Test of Computers* 27(1):10–25, 2010. Canonical Trojan taxonomy and detection survey, including side-channel-based detection families. [DOI 10.1109/MDT.2010.7](https://doi.org/10.1109/MDT.2010.7)
- **Hardware Trojan Detection by Multiple-Parameter Side-Channel Analysis** — Seetharam Narasimhan, Dongdong Du, Rajat Subhra Chakraborty, Somnath Paul, Francis G. Wolff, Christos A. Papachristou, Kaushik Roy, Swarup Bhunia. *IEEE Trans. Computers* 62(11):2183–2195, 2013. Uses multiple side-channel parameters including power to detect Trojans; the representative "PDN/power-as-evidence" detection line.
- **Role of Power Grid in Side Channel Attack and Power-Grid-Aware Secure Design** — Xinmu Wang, Wen Yueh, Debapriya Basu Roy, Seetharam Narasimhan, Yu Zheng, Saibal Mukhopadhyay, Debdeep Mukhopadhyay, Swarup Bhunia. *DAC* 2013. Directly establishes that power-grid characteristics modulate side-channel leakage and that grid design choices are security-relevant — the strongest motivation for treating PDN parameters as security-sensitive information.
- **Silicon Echoes: Non-Invasive Trojan and Tamper Detection using Frequency-Selective Impedance Analysis** — Tahoura Mosavirik, Saleh Khalaj Monfared, Maryam Saadat-Safa, Shahin Tajik. *IACR TCHES* 2023(4):238–261. Injects RF signals into the PDN and measures the "echo" (scattering parameters) to detect Trojans and P&R modifications without activating them; demonstrates that PDN impedance profiles are extractable, tamper-sensitive signatures — the direct defensive counterpart to our grid-parameter extraction. [DOI 10.46586/tches.v2023.i4.238-261](https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/11165)
- **ScatterVerif: Verification of Electronic Boards Using Reflection Response of Power Distribution Network** — Tahoura Mosavirik, Fatemeh Ganji, Patrick Schaumont, Shahin Tajik. *ACM JETC*, 2022. On-chip PDN impedance sensing for board-level tamper verification; same impedance-of-PDN measurement principle at a different scale.
- **PDNPulse: Sensing PCB Anomaly with the Intrinsic Power Delivery Network** — Huifeng Zhu, Haoqi Shan, Dean Sullivan, Xiaolong Guo, Yier Jin, Xuan Zhang. *arXiv:2204.02482*, 2022 (accepted by *IEEE TIFS* 2023). Detects board modifications via PDN impedance-profile changes; shows PDN characteristics are an intrinsic, inevitably perturbed hardware fingerprint — symmetric to our claim that true grids are distinguishable from decoys via geometry. [DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2023.3285490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02482)
- **Red Team vs. Blue Team: A Real-World Hardware Trojan Detection Case Study Across Four Modern CMOS Technology Generations** — Endres Puschner, Thorben Moos, Steffen Becker, Christian Kison, Amir Moradi, Christof Paar. *IEEE S&P* 2023, pp. 56–74. End-to-end detection case study across technology generations; grounds the practical constraints any PDN-based defense must satisfy.

## 5. Security-aware physical design / split manufacturing / layout obfuscation / camouflaging

- **Regaining Trust in VLSI Design: Design-for-Trust Techniques** — Jeyavijayan Rajendran, Vinayaka Jyothi, Ozgur Sinanoglu, Ramesh Karri. *Proceedings of the IEEE* 102(8):1266–1282, 2014. Broad design-for-trust survey covering split manufacturing, logic locking, and camouflaging; the canonical overview to anchor our defense in the established design-for-trust taxonomy.
- **Securing Computer Hardware Using 3D Integrated Circuit (IC) Technology and Split Manufacturing for Obfuscation** — Frank Imeson, Ariq Emtenan, Siddharth Garg, Mahesh V. Tripunitara. *USENIX Security* 2013, pp. 495–510 (Best Student Paper). Formalizes split-manufacturing security (k-security) by withholding BEOL wiring from the untrusted foundry; the dominant paradigm of structural obfuscation at the layout level.
- **Is Split Manufacturing Secure?** — Jeyavijayan Rajendran, Ozgur Sinanoglu, Ramesh Karri. *DATE* 2013, pp. 1259–1264. Demonstrates ML/proximity attacks that reconstruct split BEOL connectivity; the canonical example of "structural ambiguity can be broken by learning from geometry" — directly motivates our stronger, intent-level ambiguity via decoy grids.
- **Security Analysis of Logic Obfuscation** — Jeyavijayan Rajendran, Youngok Pino, Ozgur Sinanoglu, Ramesh Karri. *DAC* 2012, pp. 83–89. SAT-based analysis of XOR/XNOR logic locking; the standard reference for the logic-level counterpart to our layout-level ambiguity.
- **Security Analysis of Integrated Circuit Camouflaging** — Jeyavijayan Rajendran, Youngok Pino, Ozgur Sinanoglu, Ramesh Karri. *ACM CCS* 2013. Camouflaged standard cells that make NAND/NOR layouts indistinguishable under microscopy — the cell-level analog of our grid-level decoys (ambiguity by construction).
- **Development of a Layout-Level Hardware Obfuscation Tool** — Shweta Malik, Georg T. Becker, Christof Paar, Wayne P. Burleson. *IEEE ISVLSI* 2015, pp. 204–209. Automated tool for layout-level obfuscation; demonstrates integration of obfuscation into the physical design flow, paralleling how decoy grids would be emitted by pdngen.
- **CAPTIVE: Constrained Adversarial Perturbations to Thwart IC Reverse Engineering** — Amir Hosein Afandizadeh Zargari, Marzieh AshrafiAmiri, Minjun Seo, Sai Manoj Pudukotai Dinakarrao, Mohammed E. Fouda, Fadi Kurdahi. *Information* 14(12):656, 2023. Adds DRC-compliant adversarial perturbations to layout images to defeat CNN-based gate recognition (RecoG-Net); the most direct prior art to our "defense must respect physical design constraints" argument — our decoy grids are similarly DRC/LVS-legal by construction. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/14/12/656)

## 6. PDN obfuscation / anti-RE specifically for power grids

No solid prior work found. Searches for "power distribution network obfuscation," "PDN anti-reverse-engineering," "decoy power grid," and "power grid camouflaging" returned no peer-reviewed publications addressing obfuscation of the on-chip PDN structure itself; related hits concern electrical-power-system watermarking or side-channel-secure PDN design (Wang et al., DAC 2013; Mosavirik et al., TCHES 2023), not anti-RE. Dummy fill / metal fill literature addresses CMP planarity, not security. This appears to be a genuine gap, supporting the novelty claim of the decoy-grid defense.

## Coverage gaps

- **PDN-specific anti-RE**: nothing found — listed as an honest novelty claim above.
- **Grid-parameter recovery as an attack**: ReGDS is the only close analog (GDSII→netlist, 100% recovery), but no prior work inverts layout geometry back into *generator parameters* (pitch/offset/width/specify_grid configurations).
- **Area 2 is thin** (3 entries): image-to-netlist ML work is usually framed as RE tooling (Degate, CAPTIVE's RecoG-Net) rather than parameter recovery; additional candidates (e.g., deep-learning standard-cell recognition papers such as Dai & Brayton, HOST 2017, or Fayyazi et al., DATE 2019) were not individually verified and are marked as future additions.
- **Anti-camouflaging attacks**: the "Is Split Manufacturing Secure?" entry covers structural recovery; oracle-guided SAT attacks on camouflaged circuits (e.g., El Massad et al., HOST 2015) were not verified this pass and can be added if needed.
